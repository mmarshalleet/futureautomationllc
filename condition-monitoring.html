Condition Monitoring & Predictive Maintenance

Predictive maintenance doesn’t start with new sensors. It starts with the data you already have.

Most facilities are already sitting on years of reliability data in their parts room, CMMS, and maintenance history. Replacement frequency, emergency part usage, lead times, and repeat failures all tell a story — and that story can be used to predict what’s about to fail next.

Future Automation LLC helps manufacturers turn existing maintenance and storeroom data into actionable condition monitoring insights, then layers in targeted sensing only where it delivers real value.

Using Parts Room Data as a Predictive Signal

Your parts room already tracks early indicators of equipment health, whether it was intended to or not. We analyze trends such as:

  • Repeated bearing, seal, motor, or gearbox replacements on the same assets
  • Shortening time-between-failure for specific components
  • High-usage or frequently out-of-stock spare parts
  • Emergency pulls versus planned maintenance usage
  • Long lead-time parts that consistently extend downtime

When correlated with runtime, production schedules, and failure history, these patterns often reveal degradation weeks or months before a breakdown occurs.

Condition Monitoring That Fits Real Plants

Not every asset needs sensors. Not every failure is mechanical. Our approach prioritizes fast time-to-value by starting with what’s already known, then validating risk with targeted monitoring where it matters.

  • Vibration monitoring for motors, gearboxes, conveyors, and rotating equipment
  • Temperature and thermal monitoring for bearings, electrical cabinets, and drives
  • Electrical and motor signature analysis for early fault detection
  • Ultrasonic and acoustic monitoring for leaks, cavitation, and valve issues
  • Process and performance trend analysis tied to maintenance history

What You Gain

  • Earlier fault detection without waiting for catastrophic failure
  • Reduced unplanned downtime and emergency maintenance
  • Lower spare parts inventory through risk-based stocking
  • Improved OEE and production consistency
  • Maintenance decisions backed by data — not guesswork

How We Implement

  1. Review parts room, CMMS, and maintenance history
  2. Identify repeat-failure assets and high-risk components
  3. Rank assets by downtime, safety, and production impact
  4. Apply condition monitoring only where it provides measurable return
  5. Deliver clear, actionable maintenance guidance — not dashboards for show
